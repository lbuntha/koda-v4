Read first. Then answer. Then spell.

Word Trace teaches reading and spelling from one piece of content: a short story. The learner reads it, answers questions about it, then spells words that came from it.

A developer writes the story once. The app drafts the questions, checks them mechanically, and a person approves them. Nothing reaches a child that has not passed both.

The home screen has two tabs. Lessons are cards grouped by category; a card opens the play screen. Books is a catalog like a children’s reading library: covers on shelves, category chips, search, and a book page. A book opens Read, then the quiz. Both end at the same ring of letters.

How to review this page — three clicks.
  1. The flow — play a lesson card, then open the Books tab and read one. Try a wrong answer, a hint and Read again. Switch to Khmer. Watch the log fill in.
  2. Content Studio — run the pipeline: pick an AI provider, paste a story, generate, edit, publish. Then find your book in the Books tab and play it.
  3. Passage & checks — the data a story becomes, and the eight rules, live.

Learning log — everything this round sends

How to read it. One row per event, with the time since the round began. Response is measured from present to answered. An answer is assisted if any help was used on that question first. Practice ranking counts only unassisted first-attempt answers of 700ms or more, and needs eight of them.

Reading rules

  • Reading is not a question. It has no verdict and sends no answered. The round starts when the quiz does.
  • Sentence-level highlight, not word-by-word. Word karaoke needs a timestamp for every word of every recording. Khmer has no reliable device voice, so recordings are the only audio.
  • A word with no recording has no speaker. Text only — never a dead button.
  • “I’m ready” waits for the last sentence to be reached, never for listening.

Quiz rules

  • Read again is always there, and it is logged as help. A quiz that hid the story would test memory, not reading.
  • A choice question never reveals its answer. Hints: a nudge, then the evidence sentence highlighted, then one wrong choice ruled out.
  • Wrong stays wrong. The pressed choice is locked with a ✕ (not colour alone) and the question does not move on.
  • “First try” means no wrong answer, no hint and no Read again. Results and the parent summary use exactly that.
The letter ring — rules that are already decided
  • Trace or tap or type. Drag, sequential taps and keyboard focus + Enter all build the same word.
  • Backtrack by returning. Dragging back onto the previous tile unlocks the last one.
  • Release submits. A partial word drops the trace and costs nothing.
  • Start over appears once a tile is locked and is never an attempt.
  • Touch floor 44px. The ring shrinks before the tile does.
  • The target word — one answered, round advances.
  • A real word that isn’t the target — a bonus, never an error.
  • A non-word — one wrong attempt, the question stays.
  • No coins. Helps are free, unlimited and logged.

Content Studio — how a developer builds it

Pick an AI provider, write or paste a story, generate the content, review and edit it, then publish. The new book appears in the learner catalog, where a child reads it, answers, and spells.

AI provider→Write or paste the story→Generate→Review & modify→Publish→Learners read · answer · spell

    Who does what

    • The developer writes or pastes the story, fixes the word splits, and approves every question.
    • The model drafts questions and, if asked, a first draft of the story. It never publishes.
    • The app re-checks all of it with plain code — the eight checks in the next tab. A failed check blocks Publish.

    What Publish freezes

    • The text, the approved word splits, the questions, the pictures and the recordings — one immutable revision.
    • Re-editing makes revision 2. A child mid-quiz finishes on revision 1, so the answer key can never move under them.
    • Recordings are made at publish. A round that needs the network to say a word fails on a bus.

      Two ways in, one pipeline. Both end at the same review table, and neither reaches a child until a person presses publish.

      Way in 1 · A topic

      Pick one of the ready topics, or type your own. Choose a language and a size, press generate. The model proposes the words and writes a clue for each — fastest way to fill a pack, and the clues are invented rather than met.

      Way in 2 · Your own sentences

      Paste or upload a plain-text file — a page of a reader, a week of class sentences, a parent's own writing. The app pulls the spellable words out of the sentences, and each sentence becomes that word's clue. Nothing is invented; the context is the one the child already met.

      From sentences: what happens to the text

      • Split into sentences, then into words. English splits on whitespace. Khmer has no spaces, so it goes through Intl.Segmenter("km", {granularity:"word"}) — ICU's dictionary breaker — and the admin can correct any split in review. Try it in the Khmer section below.
      • Keep the spellable ones. Every candidate must pass the same verifier a themed word passes: a re-derived pattern, 2–8 tiles, in the bundled child lexicon, spellable from its own ring. A word that fails is listed with its reason, not dropped in silence.
      • The sentence becomes the clue, with the word replaced by a gap. One word per sentence; a sentence with four good words becomes four questions, each showing the whole sentence.
      • Tag by pattern, not by order. The uploaded text has no curriculum in it. Each extracted word is tagged with the pattern it actually matches, so it lands in whichever lessons teach that pattern.
      • Limits. Plain text or .txt, 200KB, 2000 sentences, one language per upload. No markup, no formulas, no embedded files — the upload is read as text and nothing else.
      • Uploaded text is untrusted content. It is somebody's writing on its way to a child's screen, so it takes the same review gate as a generated pack, and any instruction-looking text inside it is data, never a prompt.
      POST /api/themes/ingest // text/plain, ≤200KB "The cat sat on the warm step. Two small eggs lay in the nest." → { "candidates":[ { "word":"cat", "pattern":"cvc_a", "tiles":["C","A","T"], "sentence":"The ___ sat on the warm step.", "source":1 }, { "word":"nest", "pattern":"blend_final", "tiles":["N","E","S","T"], "sentence":"Two small eggs lay in the ___.", "source":2 } ], "rejected":[ { "word":"warm", "why":"r_controlled_not_in_selected_patterns" }, { "word":"eggs", "why":"not_in_child_lexicon" } ] }
      A sentence is a reading load. A five-year-old cannot read the clue that is supposed to help them spell. Sentence mode is therefore recorded at publish like every other prompt, the sentence is read aloud on request, and it is only used from lesson 13 — the picture modes carry the early lessons.

      From a theme: what the operator types

      • Theme — “Fruits at the market”. Free text, 120 chars.
      • Language — English or Khmer. Decides the tile rule, the lexicon and the voice.
      • Patterns — which spelling patterns this pack should feed. Multi-select from the closed list the lessons use.
      • Size — 12, 24 or 48 words.
      • Art — draw each word through /api/art/generate in house style, or pick from the existing library.

      What the server returns

      POST /api/themes/draft { "theme":"fruits", "language":"km", "patterns":["coeng_cluster"], "count":24 } → { "draft":[ { "word":"ស្វាយ", "gloss":"mango", "tiles":["ស្វ","ា","យ"], "clue":"ផ្លែឈើពណ៌លឿង ផ្អែម", "artPrompt":"a ripe mango, house style" }, … ], "rejected":[ {"word":"…","why":"pattern_mismatch"} ] }

      What the app checks before a person ever sees it

      • The pattern is re-derived, never trusted. The model says a word is magic-e; the verifier decides. §7 of the build guide: content correctness is checked independently of the generator's claim.
      • Tiles are re-segmented locally from the word itself. A model-supplied tile array is a hint, not an answer.
      • The wheel can spell it — every tile of the target appears in the offered multiset.
      • Length and legibility — 2–8 tiles; a 9-tile word does not fit a 360px wheel.
      • Blocklist and allowlist — the word is in the bundled child lexicon for its language, or it is held for explicit approval.
      • No duplicate of a word already published in that language.
      The pack is frozen on publish. Words, tiles, clue text, artwork and recorded audio are written into one immutable revision. Re-generating makes revision 2; a device on revision 1 keeps playing revision 1 until it syncs. A pack that could change under a child mid-round is a pack that can contradict its own answer key.
      Audio is recorded at publish, not spoken at play. Every published word gets a clip through the existing voice pipeline, cached with the pack. A round that needs the network to say a word is a round that fails on the bus.

      Passage

      The data

      evidence is the field that does the work: it powers hint rung 2 and check 1. words is the approved split, so tap-a-word never guesses at Khmer boundaries. In the real format each word also carries its recording and picture.

      The eight checks

        Rule 2 was rewritten while building this. The first draft said every wrong choice must come from the passage. A “who” question has only one or two people in it, so that produced silly choices (“Who went with Sokha? — the mango”). The rule is now: at least one wrong choice uses the passage’s own words, the rest are the same kind of thing as the answer, and a person confirms them.

        The trace is a band, not a rope

        Three damped springs, one requestAnimationFrame loop, a fixed 1/120s step with an accumulator so the feel does not change with the frame rate. Nothing per-frame touches React state; positions are written to the SVG through refs.

        1 · A band, not a rope

        Between locked tiles the trace is a straight line, because that is what a child is drawing. The first build here was a verlet rope with sag, and on a ring it tangles: turn a corner and the chain folds back over itself. Straight is not a simplification, it is the correct read.

        2 · Lag and bend

        The live segment is one quadratic curve. Its tip chases the finger on a stiff spring, so it trails; its control point rides a second spring driven by the perpendicular component of finger velocity, capped at 20px, so it bows slightly away from a turn and settles — and can never cross itself.

        3 · Tile springs

        Every tile is a damped spring on scale and on radial offset. Locking kicks it outward and it settles; a wrong word kicks every locked tile inward at once, which reads as a flinch.

        Stiffness of the spring the drawn tip chases the finger with. Low lags far behind; high is glued to the finger.

        Tile spring k. Damping is held at the critical ratio so it never oscillates twice.

        How much of the pointer's velocity bends the live segment backwards.

        Numbers to ship

        • step 1/120 s, accumulator capped at 4 steps — a backgrounded tab resumes instead of exploding.
        • Tip spring k 1700, ratio 0.92 — a little give, not a lag you wait for.
        • Bend spring k 300, cap ±20px; the curve is one Q, so a 9-tile word is still one path string per frame.
        • captureRadius 0.62 × tile size, and a tile already locked recaptures only if it is the previous one.
        • Tile spring: k 420, c = 2√k ≈ 41 — critically damped, one overshoot of about 6%.
        • Shuffle: angular spring k 90, c 12, stagger 22ms by index.

        Restraint is the point

        • prefers-reduced-motion — the bend and the lag go to zero, springs become instant, shuffle becomes a cross-fade. The game is identical; only the delight is dropped. Use useMotionOK from kit/motion.ts, never a private media query.
        • Reduced-motion is not fewer features. The order badges, the capture, the bonus tray and the sounds all stay.
        • No physics library. Three damped springs and one Bézier. motion is already allowed and handles the tile springs; the trail does not need a dependency.
        • Battery. The loop stops when no tile is off its rest position and nothing is being dragged — an idle wheel costs nothing.
        Never hand-tune a spring in a skill. docs/THEME.md gives the motion vocabulary — SPRING, stagger, idleFloat. The rope constants above live in one internal/tracePhysics.ts, the way additionLayout.ts centralises apparatus sizes, not scattered through the component.

        Khmer is not English with different glyphs

        This is the largest technical risk in the feature and it is worth being exact about. English spells in letters; Khmer spells in clusters. A wheel that offers Unicode code points would offer a child a subscript mark with no consonant to sit under — a tile that cannot be drawn, let alone read.

        Try it

        Type or paste a Khmer word. The segmenter below is the one the spec proposes, running live.

        And a Khmer sentence has no spaces

        An uploaded English sentence splits on whitespace. A Khmer one cannot. This is Intl.Segmenter("km", {granularity:"word"}) — ICU's dictionary breaker — running live on whatever you type.

        The rule

        tile := CONSONANT (COENG CONSONANT)* // base + subscripts | DEPENDENT_VOWEL | SIGN // its own tile COENG = U+17D2 // binds forward, never alone CONSONANT = U+1780–U+17A2 INDEP_VOWEL = U+17A5–U+17B3 // a tile on its own DEP_VOWEL = U+17B6–U+17C5 SIGN = U+17C6–U+17D1, U+17DD
        • A vowel tile draws its own dotted circle — do not add one. ា The shaper inserts U+25CC for an unattached sign by itself. Putting one in the string as well renders two circles — which is exactly what the first build of this page did, and what the screenshot caught.
        • Intl.Segmenter is not enough on its own. Grapheme clusters split some sign sequences the way a reader would not. The rule above runs first; the segmenter is the fallback for anything outside the Khmer block.
        • Compare on the string, not the tiles. The answer is correct when the joined tiles equal the word in NFC. Two different tilings of the same word are both right.
        • Font is a dependency, not a preference. Noto Sans Khmer ships with the pack; the system font on an old Android renders subscripts as boxes.
        • No spaces between words. Clue sentences cannot be split on whitespace for line breaking — use overflow-wrap:anywhere plus the segmenter.
        • Voice. Khmer device TTS is rare and usually absent. Khmer packs are recorded at publish; if a clip is missing the Listen button is not shown, rather than shown and silent.

        Standards. English lessons carry the CCSS codes they actually assess — RF.1.3.B, L.1.2.D, RF.1.3.C, L.2.2.D — primary first. The Khmer lessons carry [].         Three decisions for you

        Each has a default so nothing is blocked, but each changes what gets built.

        1 · Skill or module? — decided

        Koda Library is a new module, not a skill. Books are runtime content; lessons are bundled and gated. The spelling-pattern lessons stay a skill on a shared ring. Word Trace is now the name of the ring game.

        2 · Is “Read again” allowed in the quiz?

        Assumed: yes, logged as help. The alternative hides the story and turns a reading tool into a memory test. The cost is that an answer given after re-reading no longer counts as “first try”.

        3 · Who writes the first three passages?

        Needed before anything is built. Two English and one Khmer, by a person, and the Khmer by a native reader. The Khmer in this mock was written by me and has not been reviewed.

        Offline

        • A published passage — text, pictures, recordings — is one downloadable unit in the local store.
        • Play reads only the local store. No fetch on the play path.
        • Sync is versioned and deadlined; on timeout the answer is what the device already holds.
        • A bundled starter passage means a device that has never been online still has a shelf.

        Telemetry

        • startLesson when the quiz begins, not when Read opens.
        • present / answered per question, with expected. Task kinds: comprehension_choice, vocab_match, spell_word_in_sentence — they never average together.
        • supportUsed for each hint rung and for Read again.
        • Those same records are what the planned parent summary reads. Nothing new is built for it.
        Silent failures to check. requires takes conceptKeys, not lesson ids. params.level contiguous from 1. Practice is params.question.practice:true. iconTone is one of amber, cyan, indigo, purple, pink, emerald. Register in registry.ts and course.json. voice:plan must report 0 missing.
